Prime tower of a Bambu Lab change

ideagcode
Source
Bambu Studio
What zslice uses
The order of a change (change, flush, prime and wipe on a tower at the z of the layer, then the part), the prime volume, the grid layers of the tower, the idle nozzle of a head with two nozzles, and the reheat after the cooling before the tower.
License
AGPL-3.0
Version
Bambu Studio 02.09.01.70
Used in
src/gcode.zig, src/tower.zig, src/plate_job.zig, src/render/view.zig

The prime tower and the filament change of Bambu Studio, as a .gcode.3mf of the user's X2D shows them (Bambu Studio 02.09.01.70, two filaments on two nozzles, 19 November 2026):

  • The order of a change: the old filament prints its tower layer ("CP TOOLCHANGE START"), the change G-code (M620, T, M621) flushes into the chute, the nozzle goes to the tower at the z of the layer, a short line and a wipe, then the prime rows ("CP_TOOLCHANGE_WIPE"), a 2 mm wipe back along the last row with the retraction, then the part.
  • The prime of the new filament on the tower: filament_prime_volume = 45 mm³.
  • A switch of the nozzles flushes nothing (L0 in M620.10): each nozzle holds its own filament. The flush matrix of a nozzle counts only the changes in that nozzle.
  • The tower grows on each layer (wipe_tower_no_sparse_layers = 0). A layer with no change prints its walls and a sparse grid ("CP EMPTY GRID") of lines across the rows, about 9 mm apart.
  • The change G-code sets the new nozzle filament_cooling_before_tower (10 °C) below its temperature (M620.15 C). After the first lines on the tower, the slicer sets the print temperature again ("M104 ... ;Wipe tower reheat before wipe").
  • The idle nozzle of a head with two nozzles: M104 T (the firmware index of the nozzle, physical_extruder_map) to filament_preheat_temperature_delta (10 °C) below its temperature when the print uses it again soon ("Multi extruder pre heating"), or off for a long wait.

License: AGPL-3.0 (the method and the values, no code)

Used in: src/gcode.zig (Emitter.changeTool, towerPurge, towerFinish, towerWipe, tower_prime_mm3, idle_nozzle_delta), src/plate_job.zig (PlateJob.towers), src/render/view.zig (View.loadTower)

How zslice uses it: The same order of a change, the prime of 45 mm³, no flush at a switch of the nozzles, a tower that grows on each layer up to its last change, the print temperature after M620.15, and the idle nozzle at 10 °C below its temperature (zslice does not turn it off: it does not know the time to the next use). The layers of the tower without a prime have sparse lines of zslice's own pattern (see "Rib wall prime tower" below).

Rib wall prime tower

ideagcode
Source
Bambu Studio
What zslice uses
The shape of the rib wall tower (the box and two strips along its diagonals, the extra rib length that shrinks to the top, the least depth for the height, the fillets of 2 mm), and its settings.
License
AGPL-3.0
Link
https://github.com/bambulab/BambuStudio/blob/8e524a39d4c4cd0541801c1c46d2f77debb0bf1d/src/libslic3r/GCode/WipeTower.cpp
Version
commit 8e524a39d4c4cd0541801c1c46d2f77debb0bf1d
Used in
src/tower.zig, src/plate_job.zig, src/settings.zig

The rib wall of the prime tower of Bambu Studio, in src/libslic3r/GCode/WipeTower.cpp and src/libslic3r/PrintConfig.cpp of https://github.com/bambulab/BambuStudio, commit 8e524a39d4c4cd0541801c1c46d2f77debb0bf1d (17 November 2026), and a photo of a Bambu Studio tower of the user (22 November 2026):

  • WipeTower::generate_rib_polygon: the wall is the union of the box of the tower and two strips along its diagonals. Each strip is prime_tower_rib_width wide (8 mm). Each strip is as long as the diagonal plus the extra length, and the part past the diagonal shrinks in a straight line to 0 at the top of the tower.
  • WipeTower::plan_tower_new: the rib length is the diagonal, or the diagonal of the least depth for the height of the tower (min_depth_per_height: 5 mm at 5 mm, 20 mm at 100 mm, 40 mm at 250 mm, 60 mm at 350 mm), plus prime_tower_extra_rib_length (0 mm), never less than the diagonal. The rib width is at most half the side of the tower. A tower with ribs is square.
  • WipeTower::rounding_polygon: with prime_tower_fillet_wall, each corner of the wall that turns by 30° to 150° gets an arc that starts 2 mm before the corner.
  • The settings prime_tower_rib_wall (on), prime_tower_rib_width, prime_tower_extra_rib_length, and prime_tower_fillet_wall (on), with their meanings.
  • The photo: a wall of 4 or 5 loops, and a block of zigzag rows from wall to wall for each filament.

License: AGPL-3.0 (the method and the values, no code)

Used in: src/tower.zig (Layer.loopAt, ribOf, leastDepth, roundCorners, fillet_mm), src/plate_job.zig (PlateJob.towers: a square tower with ribs), src/settings.zig (project.prime_tower_rib_wall, prime_tower_rib_width, prime_tower_extra_rib_length, prime_tower_fillet_wall)

How zslice uses it: The same outline, rib length, and fillets. zslice finds the outline from the three convex shapes along rays from the center, and moves the fillets in for each inner loop. Bambu Studio prints one wall loop and a chamfer of brim loops that gets one loop less on each layer; zslice prints project.prime_tower_wall_loops loops (4) on each layer. Bambu Studio fills the layers without a prime with a sparse grid; zslice puts a solid base under the rows of the next prime, and sparse lines that are close at the walls, on the rest.

License: Public documents and tools; the formats and messages, no code

How zslice uses it: zslice finds Bambu printers on the network, writes the file they print, and makes the messages that start a print and read its status. The temperatures (nozzle_temper, bed_temper, chamber_temper, the targets, and the packed device.extruder.info[].temp of the printers with two nozzles, as ha-bambulab reads it). The camera: RTSP over TLS on port 322 at /streaming/live/1 (X1, H2, P2S, X2D, with "LAN Only Liveview" on), or JPEG frames over TLS on port 6000 after an 80-byte login (P1, A1), as ha-bambulab (MIT) and open-bambu-networking (https://github.com/ClusterM/open-bambu-networking) describe them. Status and RTSPS camera read from a real X2D in November 2026; the upload and the start are not yet tested with a real printer.

Top surface: no plastic on the moves between the lines

ideainfill
Source
Bambu Studio
What zslice uses
The top surface pattern monotonicline of Bambu Studio moves from one line to the next with no plastic, so a turn at the wall adds no dot.
License
AGPL-3.0
Link
https://github.com/bambulab/BambuStudio
Version
Bambu Studio 02.09.01.70 (the G-code of a user file, plate_1.gcode)
Used in
src/layer.zig, src/infill.zig

The G-code of a Bambu Studio 02.09.01.70 project (X2D, top_surface_pattern = monotonicline): in each ; FEATURE: Top surface block, the moves between two lines have no E, and the plastic of each line stops about 0.19 mm before the turn.

License: AGPL-3.0 (the method, read from G-code; no code)

Used in: src/layer.zig (thinLinks), src/infill.zig (tour)

How zslice uses it: The skin of zslice joins its lines at the walls into zigzags, and the retractless skin joins the zigzags with walks along the walls. A link printed with the full line width put twice the plastic on the inner half of its bead, where the line ends are: a dot at each turn and a ridge along each walk. zslice keeps the links (they save the travel and the retraction), but prints them with half the bead area: the plastic for each area at the edge of the skin is then the same as in the middle. Bambu Studio puts no plastic on the links of its top surface.

The wipe and the lift of a travel

ideagcode
Source
Bambu Studio
What zslice uses
The straight wipe of zslice goes along the start of a closed path and back along an open path, as the wipe of Bambu Studio. A tree support travel to another island retracts, as in Bambu Studio.
License
AGPL-3.0
Link
https://github.com/bambulab/BambuStudio
Used in
src/gcode.zig

The form of the retraction before a travel in the G-code of Bambu Studio 02.09.01.70 for the X2D (the user's file, 19 November 2026): a wipe of wipe_distance along the start of a closed path (over its seam) or back along an open path, with most of the retraction on it; then the travel with a lift of z_hop (a slope, or a spiral G3 Z over an overhang, for "Auto Lift"). In that file 2890 of 2973 retractions lift; the others are at the layer changes. zslice took the path of its straight wipe (Emitter.wipeLine) from it. It also took the rule of the support travels: a travel inside one support island has no retraction, and a travel to another island retracts (GCode::needs_retraction in src/libslic3r/GCode.cpp, commit 8e524a39d4c4cd0541801c1c46d2f77debb0bf1d, tests if a support island contains the whole travel; zslice tests the box and the loop of each tree branch, Emitter.sameSupportIsland). Its lift stays a straight G0 Z up and down, and it adds its own wipe on a small circle inside the part.

License: AGPL-3.0 (the license of Bambu Studio). zslice copied no code: only the path of the wipe, from the G-code, and the rule of the support travels.

Text: LICENSE

Notes: The values of the presets are in "Bambu Lab printer facts and G-code templates".
